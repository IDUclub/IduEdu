Intermodal graphs¶

Overview¶

An intermodal graph lets a route walk to a stop, wait, ride, change and walk on to the destination. It joins a walk graph and a public-transport graph at the platforms: every platform is attached to the nearest walkable street, so the shortest path decides by itself where walking ends and riding begins.

Function

Result

get_intermodal_graph()

Builds the walk graph and the OSM public-transport graph of a territory and joins them

join_pt_walk_graph()

Joins graphs that already exist, for example a GTFS graph (Public transport from GTFS) and a walk graph

 
intermodal = iduedu.get_intermodal_graph(osm_id=1114252, pt_kwargs={"transport_types": ["bus", "tram", "subway"]})

walk = iduedu.get_walk_graph(osm_id=1114252)
gtfs = iduedu.get_gtfs_public_transport_graph("feed.zip", crs=walk.crs)
intermodal = iduedu.join_pt_walk_graph(gtfs, walk, max_dist=30)

Inputs¶

osm_id or territory, clip_by_territory, osm_edge_tags

Passed on to both builders (see Data sources and Streets: drive, walk and bike).

walk_kwargs and pt_kwargs

Further arguments of get_walk_graph() (walk_speed, simplify) and of get_public_transport_graph() (transport_types, transport_registry). They override the shared arguments.

max_dist

The largest distance, in metres, from a platform to the walk graph: 30 m in get_intermodal_graph() and 20 m in join_pt_walk_graph(). Platforms farther away stay unconnected and are reported in the log.

add_link_edge

True (the default) adds a walking edge from the platform to the street; False places the platform on the street itself.

keep_largest_subgraph

Keep the largest connected component of the joined graph. Public-transport nodes dropped with smaller components are logged by type, because they mean that a mode lost its connection to the streets.

Both graphs must be in the same CRS. get_intermodal_graph() builds them for the same territory, so they are; for join_pt_walk_graph() pass the CRS of the walk graph to the second builder.

Method¶

  1. The two graphs are built; get_intermodal_graph() runs both builders in parallel.

  2. The walk graph is made directed, with every walking edge in both directions, because public-transport edges have a direction.

  3. Every platform-like node of the public-transport graph (platform, subway_entry_exit, station_entry_exit) is projected onto the nearest walking edge within max_dist with project_objects2urban_graph(). The edge is split at the projection point, and with add_link_edge a link edge at walking speed joins the platform to that point.

  4. Projected platforms take the ids of their walk nodes and keep their attributes; the other public-transport nodes get new ids after those of the walk graph. Public-transport edges are renumbered to match, and the two edge tables are joined.

  5. With keep_largest_subgraph the largest connected component is kept.

Waiting is part of the graph: a boarding edge from a platform to a route carries the wait of that route, so a change between routes costs a walk between platforms, where needed, and a new wait (Public transport from OpenStreetMap).

Outputs¶

A directed UrbanGraph of type intermodal whose edges have type walk or a public-transport type, and oneway. Every walking edge is stored once per direction. Routing functions work on it like on any other graph, by time_min for travel times. For Vasilyevsky Island the joined graph has 23 665 nodes and 32 329 edges, with all 251 platforms attached to the streets.

Functions¶

get_intermodal_graph

Build an intermodal (PT+walking) UrbanGraph for a territory.

join_pt_walk_graph

Join public-transport and pedestrian UrbanGraph objects into an intermodal graph.

Limitations¶

  • Boarding waits are averages; the graph does not know when the next departure leaves.

  • A platform farther than max_dist from any walkable street stays unconnected and, with keep_largest_subgraph, is dropped with its routes when nothing else connects them.

  • Changes between routes at one platform are free apart from the wait; walks inside large interchanges follow the mapped stop areas and pathways only.
