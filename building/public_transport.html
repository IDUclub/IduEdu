Public transport from OpenStreetMap¶

Overview¶

get_public_transport_graph() builds the bus, trolleybus, tram, subway, monorail and share-taxi routes of a territory from the route relations of OpenStreetMap. Travel times between stops come from a speed model of every mode, and waiting times at boarding from the averages of the transport registry. For timetable travel and waiting times use a GTFS feed (Public transport from GTFS); to walk to and between stops, join the graph with a walk graph (Intermodal graphs).

pt = iduedu.get_public_transport_graph(osm_id=1114252, transport_types=["bus", "tram", "subway"])

Inputs¶

osm_id or territory

The territory, see Data sources. Every route relation that touches it is read with all its stops, also those outside; clip_by_territory=True cuts the graph at the boundary.

transport_types

The modes to build: one name, a list, or None for every mode of the registry. A requested mode must exist in the registry, otherwise the builder raises ValueError. OpenStreetMap tags some services under names the registry does not use: tram also reads route=light_rail relations and taxi reads route=share_taxi (OSM_ROUTE_ALIASES in iduedu.constants.transport_specs).

transport_registry

The speed and waiting-time parameters of every mode, DEFAULT_REGISTRY by default. Trains need a registry that defines them, such as DEFAULT_REGISTRY_W_TRAIN. See Transport registry.

Method¶

Graph structure¶

Every route gets its own chain of route stops, one node per stop of the route, connected by travel edges whose type is the mode (bus, tram, …) and whose route is the route number. Routes that stop at the same place share a platform node, the place where passengers wait. A boarding edge leads from the platform to the route stop and carries the waiting time of the mode; an alighting edge leads back at no cost. A transfer between routes therefore passes through a platform and pays the wait of the route boarded.

The subway is built from the stop areas of its stations. Station entrances (subway_entry_exit nodes) are linked to the station by subway_entrance and subway_exit edges. Their length is the slanted distance from the entrance down to the station, the straight distance combined with the depth tag of the station, and their time is that length at 0.75 m/s, the speed of an escalator. Stations of one interchange are linked by subway_transfer edges of 1.5 times their straight distance at 5 km/h. When a station lacks mapped entrances or exits, its node becomes a platform and is joined to the walk graph directly.

Travel time between stops¶

For a segment of length L between two stops, the cruising speed v is the free speed of the mode (base_speed_kmh, or v(L) below when long_speed_kmh is set), capped by vmax_tech_kmh and by the road speed limit where that is lower. With span = accel_dist_m + brake_dist_m:

  • if L >= span, the time is dwell_min + (L + span) / v: accelerating and braking cost as much as covering span twice at cruising speed;

  • if L < span, the vehicle never reaches v. Its peak speed is v * sqrt(L / span) and the time is dwell_min + 2 * L / v_peak. Both cases give the same time at L == span.

When long_speed_kmh and speed_half_length_m are set, the free speed grows with the segment length from base_speed_kmh towards long_speed_kmh:

v(L) = base_speed_kmh + (long_speed_kmh - base_speed_kmh) * L / (L + speed_half_length_m)

It is halfway at L = speed_half_length_m. Timetables of every mode show this growth: a long stretch between stops runs on roads and tracks built for speed. A posted road limit does not raise the free speed; it is honoured only where it is lower, so a motorway does not make a bus faster. Fitting the free speed as base + coef * limit against published timetables showed that the two terms cannot be told apart, and leaving the limit term out improved the accuracy for every mode measured.

Speeds and dwell times are fitted on every published GTFS segment that has its own geometry (shapes.txt), about 859 000 segments in 239 cities, minimising the error in scheduled minutes with every city weighing the same, and cross-validated on held-out cities. A free speed that grows with the segment length is used for trams and trains, where it carries over to unseen cities; buses, subways and trolleybuses keep one free speed, because the growing form does not improve them. Waiting times are medians across cities of the average-day timetable wait over regular services (06:00 to 22:00, a line counted towards the same next stop). Too few cities publish a timetable for monorails or share taxis, so these two borrow the subway and bus profiles.

A custom registry changes the speeds or the waits of the modes:

from iduedu import DEFAULT_REGISTRY, TransportRegistry, TransportSpec, get_public_transport_graph

registry = TransportRegistry({mode: DEFAULT_REGISTRY.get(mode) for mode in DEFAULT_REGISTRY.list_types()})
registry.update("bus", base_speed_kmh=32.0, avg_wait_time_min=5.0)
registry.update("tram", long_speed_kmh=60.0, speed_half_length_m=3000.0)
registry.add(TransportSpec(name="ferry", vmax_tech_kmh=40, accel_dist_m=300, brake_dist_m=300,
                           base_speed_kmh=20.0, avg_wait_time_min=15.0))

pt = get_public_transport_graph(osm_id=1114252, transport_registry=registry)

Mode names are normalised (lower case, stripped). registry.update("bus", name="express_bus") renames a mode, and registry.ensure("ferry") creates a mode with default parameters when it is missing. get_intermodal_graph() passes the registry on through pt_kwargs.

Outputs¶

A directed UrbanGraph of type public_transport. Nodes have type (platform, the mode of a route stop, subway_station, subway_platform, subway_entry_exit), route and name; subway stations also depth. Edges have type (the mode, boarding, alighting, subway_entrance, subway_exit, subway_station, subway_transfer), route, length_meter, time_min and oneway. Boarding and alighting edges have length_meter = 0. transport_stats() summarises stops, routes and stop spacing by mode.

Functions¶

get_public_transport_graph

Build a directed public-transport graph for one or multiple transport modes within a territory.

TransportSpec

Configuration of a single public-transport mode used to estimate travel time on graph edges.

TransportRegistry

Registry of available public-transport modes and their specifications.

Limitations¶

  • Travel and waiting times are averages of the mode, not of the route or the time of day. Use a GTFS feed for the timetable of a city (Public transport from GTFS).

  • Routes are only as complete as their relations in OpenStreetMap: a route without stops or with gaps in its ways gives a shorter or broken chain.

  • The subway model needs mapped stop areas; entrances without a depth tag on the station are taken at ground level.
